## What is your current goal? Has it changed since the proposal?
My goal is to understand which communities across the United States are vulnerable 
to natural disasters, and the socioeconomic makeup of these communities.

My goal has not changed since the proposal.

## Are there data challenges you are facing? Are you currently depending on mock data?
I initially wanted to use census tract data to add a high level of granularity to the data, 
especially when it comes to dissecting the socio-economic factors that contribute to a community's hazard risk vulnerability.
However, as I was creating the charts, I realized county-level data works best on a national level, and that tract level was too granular.

All of the charts use real data, although I am using a sample of census tracts for the demographic charts while I transition to using county-level ACS data. 

## Describe each of the provided images with 2-3 sentences to give the context and how it relates to your goal.
All visualizations are saved in the "charts" folder

Visualization 1: This choropleth map illustrates the spatial distribution of overall natural hazard risk scores across U.S. counties. By highlighting high-risk regions in dark red, it provides the macro-level baseline needed to identify which communities face the greatest physical exposure to disasters.

Visualization 2: This small-multiple map grid breaks down geographic risk profiles across 6 distinct hazard types, such as wildfires, hurricanes, and riverine flooding. It reveals how different hazards concentrate in specific geographic areas rather than following a uniform  pattern.

Visualization 3: This heatmap displays average risk scores by state across 6 distinct hazard categories, providing the audience a comparative matrix of regional vulnerability. It helps pinpoint which states experience compounding pressures across multiple natural disaster types.

Visualization 4: This scatterplot examines the relationship between census tract population size and overall risk scores on a logarithmic scale, encoded with social vulnerability. The chart shows whether densely populated communities experience higher disaster risk burdens. (This will change to a comparison of county population).

Visualization 5: This faceted scatterplot grid evaluates key socioeconomic indicators against hazard risk, including poverty, disability, unemployment, and renter occupancy rates. It directly addresses the socioeconomic makeup of vulnerable communities by showing how baseline hardships intersect with physical disaster exposure.

Visualization 6: This ranked horizontal bar chart highlights the top 15 states with the highest average county natural hazard risk. It provides a clear, categorical ranking that summarizes regional vulnerability.

Visualization 7: This smoothed density distribution plot compares the statistical shapes and risk score spreads across various hazard categories. It illustrates whether specific hazards exhibit skewed, high-risk extremes or broader baseline distributions.

Visualization 8: This ranked lollipop chart pinpoints the specific communities and counties with the highest overall risk scores, categorized visually by their social vulnerability index. It filters the data to individual geographic locations experiencing extreme socio-environmental vulnerability.
